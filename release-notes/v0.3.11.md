# Ether v0.3.11

Highlights:

- **Libraries are sources plus rules.** A library starts with everything its sources supply and is narrowed only by the rules on its **Rules** tab. Making a library from a filter just fills in its first rules, and you edit them in the same editor. Saved-filter libraries are converted when you upgrade: they keep exactly the titles they had, their sources are unchanged, and their filter now appears as a visible **Show** rule.
- **Live TV follows your channels.** Live TV is on whenever you have channels and off when you don't. The Live TV switch and the setup question are gone. With no channels, the Live TV page offers the four decade channels, IPTV, or a new virtual channel to start with. If you had turned Live TV off in v0.3.10 but kept channels, Live TV now shows again.
- **Guides split a big lineup into tabs.** Group channels into named **Guides**. By default each of your provider's channel groups is a guide; you can rename, reorder, combine or hide them. Each guide appears as its own tab in the Plex guide, as a tag in Emby and as a group in Ether's own client. The editor stays fast with 10,000 channels.
- **Search finds channels and programmes in every app.** Plex universal search, Jellyfin, Emby and the console find channels in any guide, along with programmes: titles, episodes and descriptions, from IPTV guides and from Ether's own virtual channels.
- **Describe a channel and watch it take shape.** On **New virtual channel**, choose **Describe it**. As you chat, a live card shows the channel's name, branding, what it will play and its schedule. Apply fills in the new channel's settings, and nothing is saved until you press **Create**.
- **Descriptive search uses your AI.** When you've connected a language model, a search like "cozy cute girls night in" in Plex, Jellyfin or Ether's own client is answered by the same logic that builds collections from a description. Without AI, searches match titles only.
- **Top 10 rows.** Turn on **Ranked (Top 10)** for any collection or home row, and the built-in Top 10 rows are ranked by default. Each card shows a large rank numeral beside the poster: wide cards where the app shows wide cards, and a portrait version everywhere else.
- **Trailer preview rows.** Turn on **Play trailer preview on focus** for a collection or home row. Apps that support it play the focused title's trailer, and other apps show wide artwork. Trailers download only when someone focuses a title, then stay cached.
- **Plugins get the room they need.** A hosted plugin declares the memory, time and compute it needs ([#476](https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether/issues/476)). Ether grants it up to limits you control: by default 2 GiB of memory (up to 4 GiB), 12-hour jobs, no cap on background work, and 8 GiB downloads. Jobs can run across several calls until they finish ([#475](https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether/issues/475)). Downloads are written straight to a cache ([#474](https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether/issues/474)), and ZIP members can be read in pieces ([#477](https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether/issues/477)). **Plugins › Reference** now starts with *Start here in 5 minutes*, has ten recipes, and offers **Copy for your AI assistant**.

## Playback

- **Every app sees the same versions.** Plex apps see exactly the number of versions your settings ask for, from the first screen through to Play. The count never grows or shrinks between screens, which is what made Samsung, LG and VIDAA TVs fail before playback started ([#483](https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether/issues/483), thanks @roadhazard). A version Ether hasn't found a release for yet keeps its place and is filled in when you press Play. Each version shows its real release name once it's known.
- **HEVC releases that never started** ([#491](https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether/issues/491), thanks @arjanterheegde). HEVC streams that carry their codec setup data inside the video, rather than in the file header, now play without converting the video. A file that keeps failing gets one fallback attempt and is then skipped, instead of restarting dozens of times a minute.
- **Quality switches keep playing.** When you change quality, or Ether steps down automatically, the new quality starts at the segment the player asks for and the old one keeps playing until it's ready. That removes the 7–9 second gap.
- **The first Play tries the next source** ([#463](https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether/issues/463), thanks @xSkibbles). If a debrid stream doesn't answer in time, Ether moves on to the next candidate within the same Play.
- **Plex subtitles appear on time.** Embedded text subtitles reach Plex Web and Apple TV, iPhone and iPad as the video plays, instead of waiting for the whole track to be extracted.
- **Correct channel counts for 7.1 AAC.** Ether used to report AAC tracks flagged as 7.1 as having 7 channels. They now report 8, so audio is converted correctly.
- **The right film from a multi-film pack.** When a release holds several films (for example *The Mummy* trilogy), Ether checks each file and plays the one you picked. Before, *The Mummy* (1999) could play *The Mummy Returns*. A release with a single video still plays, even when its file name doesn't match the title.
- **Faster restarts after a debrid link renews.** Ether remembers what it learned about a torrent file. When the provider issues a new link, playback no longer waits to analyse the same file again.
- **Usenet providers at their connection limit stay on.** When a provider refuses extra connections ("max number of simultaneous connections"), Ether uses fewer connections and keeps going. Before, it treated that reply as a rejected password and switched the provider off for hours. **Test** now re-checks a provider immediately.

## Activity

- **The Bitrate on a stream card is the stream's real bitrate.** Streams that play the original file used to show "—" or a momentary spike (hundreds of Mbps), because the card showed how fast Ether was sending data at that moment. The card now shows the file's bitrate.

## Cache

- **Warm what people are watching.** Warming follows a strict order: the next episodes and resume points of what people are watching, then titles you've pinned with **Keep warm**, then the newest watchlist additions, then speculative picks. Adding a show to a watchlist warms that viewer's next unwatched episode, and a movie's opening. **Cache › Edit policy** holds your Keep warm list and a switch for speculative warming.

## Quality

- **A profile's minimum is a preference** ([#471](https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether/issues/471), thanks @alcroito). Releases that meet your profile always come first. When none do, Ether plays the closest match and says so, for example "Best available — below this profile's minimum (score 6 of 200000)". Blocklisted releases, "never" formats, required languages and device limits are still never relaxed. Release scores are now consistent, and imported profiles with "Dolby Digital" and "Dolby Digital +" formats keep both.
- **Profiles behave the same everywhere.** A library's quality profile applies to playback, discovery and new-release gating alike.

## Apps

- **Jellyfin on Roku and NVIDIA Shield** ([#421](https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether/issues/421), thanks @krakenn9). Older Jellyfin Android TV apps can sign in and redeem Quick Connect, and Roku image requests and recursive browsing work.
- **Collections stay put while you scroll** ([#285](https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether/issues/285), thanks @arjanterheegde). A collection returns the same titles in the same order whether an app loads it page by page or all at once, including collections of shows. Preview rows in apps like Plezy now page through the whole collection.
- **Slow pages are faster.** On large libraries, Jellyfin episode lists and recommendations, genre and studio browsing, and artwork checks read far less data.

## Live TV

- **Channels ride out unstable sources.** Some streams keep changing their internal layout every few seconds. Ether now continues playing through those changes instead of restarting the picture each time. When a link keeps changing without showing anything, Ether switches to the channel's next link and leaves the bad one alone for two minutes.
- **No more silent channels.** Ether now plays a stream's separate audio track, picks the best-quality version that has sound instead of the lowest, and only uses a link without sound when nothing else plays.
- **IPTV through your VPN.** When Ether's outbound proxy is on (**Server › General › How Ether reaches the internet**, for example gluetun's HTTP proxy), IPTV playlists, guides and streams go through it too. Plex and your own virtual channels stay on your normal connection.
- **Several logins in one IPTV source.** Add up to 16 provider logins to one source. Each new stream uses a free login, viewers watching the same channel share one stream, and the channel list isn't duplicated.
- **Live TV uses your hardware encoder** ([#481](https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether/issues/481), thanks @rblair2), and falls back to software without restarting the stream.
- **IPTV and add-on channels stay visible.** Opening the console's channel list no longer hides add-on channels from viewers until a restart.
- **AI channel building is reliable.** When a model says it's about to propose something but doesn't make the call, Ether now asks it again so the approval card appears. If it still fails, you get a clear error with **Try again**.
- Community fixes: the Overlays switch ([#485](https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether/pull/485)), Up next and Upcoming cards ([#486](https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether/pull/486)) and clearing number fields ([#472](https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether/pull/472)), all thanks @activechay-lab. A disabled source's channels are now hidden ([#473](https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether/pull/473), thanks @jmiller0).

## Watchlists

- **Faster, attributed Plex watchlists** ([#490](https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether/pull/490), thanks @drondeseries). Plex watchlist feeds are checked every few minutes, and only re-downloaded when they've changed. Friends' items are credited to the right viewer, and one title Ether can't match no longer fails a friend's whole sync.
