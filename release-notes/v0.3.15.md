# Ether v0.3.15

Highlights:

- **Search a person's name and get their titles.** Typing "Brad Pitt" in Vela, Plex, Jellyfin, Emby or Silo now brings up his movies and shows, lead roles first, right after any exact title matches. The person appears too, with a headshot, where the app shows people. Each viewer's library access and rating limits still apply. A new name index keeps it fast on large libraries.
- **Plugin libraries work in every app.** Content a plugin adds now shows its posters and backdrops in Vela, Plex, Jellyfin, Emby and Silo. Cast and other people in a plugin's items are searchable, with headshots. Plugin titles play through converted (HLS) streams, which every one of them refused before.
- **See what happened to any play.** Activity › History rows have a **Timeline** tab: each step from request to stop with times, the releases tried, why it played direct or transcoded, failovers and stalls, the play's log lines, and the real failure cause instead of "see server logs". Ether records timelines for plays from this version on.
- **Pictures never run out, and load once.** Every app used to get a short-lived permission for each poster, logo and backdrop, and a viewer could hold only so many at once, so a busy guide or a long session left libraries, previews and Live TV without pictures or failing outright. There is no such limit any more: every picture has one permanent address that changes only when the picture does, so apps keep it in their own cache and ask for it once. Ether itself downloads each TMDB, channel and plugin image once and keeps it on disk, checks a third-party image again only after the provider's own cache time, remembers images that fail instead of retrying them on every request, and backs off a provider that asks it to slow down.
- **Signed Windows downloads.** The Windows installer and the standalone `ether.exe` and tray app are now signed with a Microsoft-verified certificate (Braedon Saunders), so Windows shows a verified publisher instead of treating them as unknown, unsigned files, which Smart App Control blocked (#527, thanks @krakenn9 for the thorough reports).
- **A cleaner console.** Sixty fixes from a review of every page: Server, Live TV, Libraries and Streaming. Pages fit the window without sideways scrolling, controls sit inside their cards, drawers keep their footers in view, and phone layouts work.

## Playback

- **Private-tracker torrents never reach debrid with your passkey.** A torrent from a private tracker carries your personal passkey in its tracker addresses, and sending it to a debrid service is how accounts get banned. Ether now recognises private torrents (marked private in the torrent, or from an indexer set up as private) and uses them on debrid only when the service already has them cached, checked by the bare info hash with no tracker list; it never asks debrid to download one. Release history says when one was skipped for this.
- **Ether identifies itself honestly to indexers.** Requests now say `Ether/0.3.15 (+https://github.com/braedonsaunders/ether)` instead of a fixed `Ether/1.0`, so indexer admins can see what Ether is and contact the project rather than ban users. The options that made Ether pretend to be Sonarr, Radarr, Prowlarr, NZBHydra2, SABnzbd or NZBGet are gone; Sources › Indexers has one free-text User agent field (and one per indexer) instead. If you had picked one of those, the field is filled with exactly what it sent.
- **A speed guess no longer outweighs your preferences.** Ether estimated your connection from how fast your last stream was read, which is just that title's own bitrate, and then ruled out every release above it, so a lower-scored release in the wrong language could win. It now measures speed only from reads that weren't waiting on the player, uses the estimate only to break ties, and steps down to a lighter release only after playback actually stalled at a similar bitrate.
- **Episodes released early on streaming services show up.** TMDB dates episodes by their TV broadcast, so a series a streaming service released all at once stayed hidden in Ether until each episode's broadcast date (Cunk on Cinema showed one of three episodes for a week). Once a season has started, opening or playing the show now checks for the rest of the season in the background. An episode appears as soon as a working release of it is confirmed (cached on your debrid service, or passing a health check), whatever TMDB's date says. Ether never shows an episode without a working release, keeps TMDB's numbering, and still turns down a different cut whose length doesn't match the episode. Each season is checked at most every six hours.
- **IPTV channels that send video in bursts stay connected.** Some providers send a few seconds of video, pause, then send more. Ether cut such a stream after 2 seconds of silence and reconnected in a loop (CNN on some providers). It now learns a stream's rhythm and waits through its pauses, up to 8 seconds, while a stream that has really stopped still fails over.
- **Obfuscated Usenet releases open.** Some uploaders scramble the size and name information in every chunk of a post. Ether trusted that information and rejected multi-volume RAR releases as truncated. It now works out each file's real size from the byte positions every chunk reports (#577, with a contribution from @thedancingdeveloper).
- **A dub-only profile prefers the dub over a higher-resolution original.** A release whose name states no audio language was treated as acceptable to every profile, so for anime and other foreign titles an untagged 4K release, almost always in the original language, outranked a 1080p English dub. Ether now presumes an untagged release is in the title's original language: with a profile set to English, a declared English dub ranks above it at any resolution, and the original still plays when no dub exists.
- **Anime plays with a dub-only profile.** For a show whose name another show shares (One Piece the anime and the 2023 live-action series), Ether rejected ordinary anime release names like "[Group] One Piece - 001" as ambiguous, and a profile requiring English audio then dropped releases whose language wasn't in the name. When the show you want is animated and the other one isn't, anime-style naming now identifies it, and a release naming the other show is still refused. A release whose audio language isn't stated is checked by reading its actual audio tracks before it plays, for up to four releases per play; one without the required language is skipped and never played as a fallback. Release history now says whether a release was the wrong show or lacked the audio language (#586).
- **Plex for Roku opens libraries again.** Current Plex for Roku apps report a four-part version (9.21.12.12191-2cb8e41a8-Plex), which Ether read as an app older than 6.4.2. It then sent libraries in the old format without the details a current app needs to open them, so Home loaded but every library showed "Ether is Currently Unavailable". Ether now reads only the major, minor and patch numbers (#571, thanks @vonyay).
- **Subtitles in Swiftfin and other Apple Jellyfin apps.** Apps that play through Apple's own player only accept subtitles inside the stream, but Ether offered embedded subtitles as separate files they never load, and making such a file read the whole release first, which on a large Usenet remux took minutes. Embedded SRT and WebVTT subtitles now arrive inside the stream, as they already did for Plex's Apple apps, and switching tracks works in the player (#592, thanks @oleedv).
- **Sorting and filters work in your own libraries in Plex.** In a library you defined yourself, Plex sent your sort and filter choices but Ether ignored them: Title, Date Added and their reverse all returned the same order, and Genre or Unwatched changed nothing. Your libraries now honour the same sorts, filters and Unwatched as the built-in ones, within the library's own titles (#588, thanks @kizit).

## Console

- **"Show in apps" says what it means.** A library's settings now show one Kind (Movies, TV shows, or a plugin's own kind) and a switch per app, with a short line saying how each app shows it ("Shown as a movie library"). The per-app type pickers, which listed different choices for every app and read as if the library appeared inside Movies, are gone. The Delete library button is red.
- **Device and app edits start from the built-in lists.** Editing a device or app for the first time shows its built-in codecs, containers, subtitles and Dolby Vision profiles ticked, so you only add what's missing. **Reset to defaults** clears your changes.
- **Live TV › Guide has two tabs.** The duplicate Guide sources tab is gone; sources live in Streaming › Sources, whose cards now show refresh, guide coverage and errors. Channel mapping fits the page width.
- **Sources show their real state.** An indexer missing its site definition or paused shows as needing attention, and a Usenet account in use from another address says so instead of reporting a wrong password.
- **Library rules preview correctly.** Rules no longer show "0 shown · 0 hidden" or error when you add one, and opening a collection briefly no longer saves a blank draft.

- **Descriptive search puts the well-known matches first.** The AI filtered correctly but sometimes ranked by date or by rating without enough votes, so "funny heist movies from the 2000s" led with obscure titles and "90s sci-fi for a family movie night" listed 1999 first. It now ranks matches by popularity unless you ask for an order such as newest or best rated; a decade is treated as a filter, not an order. Collections built with Describe it follow the same rule.
- **Descriptive search waits long enough for the AI.** A search like "funny space movies" now waits up to 20 seconds for the AI, up from 10. Typical models answer a new descriptive query in 12–15 seconds, so the first search often showed only title matches. Title searches stay instant, and a repeated descriptive search answers from cache.
- **Editing an IPTV source while it refreshes works.** Saving changes to a source, such as its groups or a new guide file, while the same source was refreshing failed at once with "Couldn't import this source". A save now waits for the refresh to finish. When a source can't be imported, the console shows Ether's actual reason instead of a generic "Check the source settings".
- **Testing a thinking model works.** The AI test in Integrations gave up after 20 seconds, the limit every other connection test uses, so models that reason before answering (Gemini's thinking models, for example) failed with "context deadline exceeded" even though they worked. The AI test now waits up to two minutes, the same limit Ether uses when it actually talks to the model (#570, thanks @drondeseries).

## Live TV

- **IPTV channels open from Plex search.** Searching in Plex found your IPTV channels, but choosing one did nothing: Ether returned them in a form Plex's apps don't read, without the details needed to tune them. Channel results now open and tune the channel, like the guide does (#593, thanks @MBProMovies).
- **Usenet doesn't block Live TV.** Without your own stream limit, Ether limits streams to what your Usenet connections can carry (20 connections allow 2), and Live TV counted against that, so two Usenet movies stopped every IPTV channel from starting. That limit now counts only streams that play a release; IPTV channels play from their own source and are never blocked by it. A Max concurrent streams you set yourself still covers everything (#561).
- **Your groups are tabs in one Plex guide.** In Plex, Live TV is one source again, and each of your groups is a tab in its guide (US, Canada, Sports…), instead of a separate source per group. Plex Web named every one of those sources after your server and repeated What's On and Featured for each.
- **Your groups are tabs in Vela's guide again.** 0.3.14 sent Vela the channel groups under a name Vela doesn't read, so its Guides tabs disappeared. Each group is a tab in Vela's guide again.
- **IPTV channels start behind a proxy.** A channel whose source answered its probe gets its own time to start (8–12 seconds, scaled by how long the probe took) instead of whatever was left of one shared 8-second budget, and the gap Ether keeps between tunes at a provider no longer eats into it. A dead source still fails over to a backup feed quickly (#563).
- **AI-built styles show real content.** Titles, times, posters and words fill in on every scene, in Create's live build and in Design, and the chat keeps its history when you switch tabs.
- **Map guides one group at a time.** Channel mapping, Auto-match and Match with AI can be limited to one of your groups or a provider group (#564, thanks @drondeseries).
- **IPTV HLS channels behind a relay start.** Ether tells ffmpeg the input is HLS before asking for the live start point, so a relayed stream whose address doesn't end in .m3u8 no longer fails with "Option live_start_index not found" (#562, thanks @drondeseries).
- **Vela's guide keeps loading.** Each guide page asked for a fresh image permission for every logo and programme picture, even ones repeated on the same page, so a viewer scrolling or refreshing the guide used up their image allowance within minutes and the whole guide failed. A page now reuses one permission per picture, and if the allowance is used up the guide still loads, without the remaining artwork.
- **Channel logos show in Plex for Android at home.** Plex's Android app, like its Apple apps, loads guide logos through Plex's cloud, which can't reach your home network, so logos only appeared away from home. Ether now gives it a logo address Plex's cloud can fetch: the channel's own public logo, or Ether's signed logo on your remote-access address.
- **Ask Ether builds a whole channel style.** A request like "an 80s synthwave movie channel" now designs every moment, from the ident, up next, tonight and breaks to off air, welcome, the lower third and the corner logo, instead of stopping after four. The AI only uses layers the renderer can draw, follows layout, type, contrast and motion guidance, and Ether repairs small mistakes in a draft instead of failing.
- **Create is one full-height chat.** In the Style editor, Create is a single conversation down the left third with the live build beside it. Keep, refine, try again and revert are small buttons in the chat, shown when they apply.
- **Share styles as plugins.** A style's Export as plugin downloads a plugin anyone can install from Plugins or the Marketplace. The separate Import pack button is gone, and the plugin guide explains how to build style plugins.
- **Channel logos show in Plezy on iPhone, iPad and Apple TV.** Ether gave Plezy the logo address meant for Plex's own Apple apps, which Plezy can't load, so every logo was blank. Plezy now gets the same logos as on Android and the web.
- **Images, video and audio upload to the On Air library.** Every upload from a style designer, a reel or the Library was refused by Ether's request check (the upload left out a required header), so the progress bar stopped partway and the file never arrived. This affected every release since v0.3.5. A failed upload now also says why, and retrying the same file gets its own progress bar. PNGs whose metadata mentions SVG (common in images exported from design tools) upload as the PNGs they are, and an SVG Ether can't draw safely says to upload it as a PNG or JPG instead.

## Plugins

- **Plugins can add poster badges.** A plugin can attach short factual badges, such as "EN DUB" or "DIRECTOR'S CUT", to titles; Ether draws them with its own poster overlays in your chosen style, and each plugin's badge set has its own switch under the poster settings' From plugins. Badges are sent ahead of time, so posters never wait on a plugin, and uninstalling the plugin restores the original posters (#594, thanks @kizit).
- **Search inside collection and people tabs.** A plugin library's collection tabs and people tabs have a search box: it ignores case and accents and matches the start of any word, keeps the tab's order, and answers in milliseconds on large libraries. Plugins can turn it off for a tab or set its placeholder text.
- **Pluto TV loads again.** The Pluto TV plugin asked for every channel with 30 hours of guide in one request, which grew past the 10 MB a plugin may fetch at once, so the plugin stopped loading. Version 1.0.2 fetches the channel list on its own and the guide in slices of a few hours, splitting any slice that is still too big (#587).
- **Collection tabs can be alphabetical, and people get their own tab.** A plugin library's collection tab can list its collections by name instead of by size, and a new people tab lists everyone in one of its people fields (cast, for example) with headshots and title counts; choosing a person opens their page with all their titles. A collections page now loads in a handful of queries instead of hundreds: about 30 ms instead of several seconds on a 35,000-title library.
- **Scheduled plugin work runs as a background job.** A plugin's scheduled task can now start a background job, as an owner action already could: lower priority, progress and cancel. A long scheduled task no longer holds up other plugins' health checks, deliveries and refreshes, and two runs of the same task never overlap (#589).
- **Owner jobs can't block playback.** Preparing a plugin title to play has its own job limit, separate from the two jobs an owner action may run, so a long catalogue build no longer leaves Play saying "preparing" for hours. If playback jobs are full, the app gets a clear "busy, try again" with the reason, and the plugin's Jobs list shows it (#590).
- **Plugins can see their storage use.** `kv.usage()` reports the bytes and keys a plugin has stored and its limits, so it can make room or warn before a save fails (#591).
- **Plugin limits are honest.** The time a plugin is told it has for a request is now the time it actually gets: 5 seconds to find a release, for example, where Ether used to stop it after 1 second (#572). Background library syncs run on the plugin's own limits, so a large first sync can finish. `runtime.limits()` reports the real limits for the current call.
- **Syncs survive rate limits and restarts.** A sync waits out a plugin's "retry after" and tries the page again. A restart that interrupted a sync no longer counts as a plugin failure, which used to switch the plugin off.
- **Release searches from plugins work better.**
  - Results come with your quality profile's score, resolution, source and codec for video releases (#581).
  - A plugin can ask an indexer for its newest releases in a category, with no search term (#583).
  - One broken indexer no longer fails the whole search.
  - Usenet results keep the indexer they came from, so their NZB can be downloaded.
- **Plugins deliver more kinds of files.** A plugin that names an exact file in a torrent gets that file even if it isn't a video, such as a ROM (#574). Files inside 7z and RAR archives stream to apps, and a compressed ZIP file streams without downloading the whole archive (#573). An item made of several files plays as one stream and can be fetched part by part (#576).
- **"Preparing", not "no source".** When a plugin is still preparing a release at the end of its time, apps are told to try again shortly instead of being told the item can't play (#580).
- **Plugin apps can sign viewers in.** Plugin apps that use a sign-in token appear in Connect apps with their own address, token and QR code (#579).
- **Plugins can reach servers you choose.** A plugin can ask to connect to addresses set in its settings. You approve or revoke each one in its permissions (#584).
- **Item limits are yours to set.** The 2 million item limit per plugin is now a setting with no upper ceiling, and the console shows each plugin's items against it (#582).
- **A passing test shows its message.** A plugin's Test result shows any message the plugin sends on success, not only on failure (#575).
- **New plugin video libraries show in Vela** by default, and every library now has its own Vela row in Show in apps.

## Vela

- **Plugins shape their libraries in Vela.** A plugin library can choose its cards (vertical posters, 16:9 landscape thumbnails or squares) and its tabs: Recommended, the full library, and collections built from one of its filters, each sorted the way the plugin chooses, newest first for example. Its declared filters and sorts appear in Vela, and numbers, dates and lengths become simple choices such as years or "10–30 min". Vela search can be limited to one library.
- **Sort and filter search results.** Vela search results can be sorted by relevance, title, release date, date added, rating or popularity, and limited to movies, shows or a plugin's content. Titles with no value for the chosen sort go last.
- **The live guide answers "unchanged".** Opening or refreshing the guide when nothing changed costs no work: the channel list, guide pages and guide windows answer "not modified" before any artwork is prepared, and a page answered that way always carries a next-page link that is still valid.
- **Big guides load in fewer requests.** Vela can now ask for up to three hours of listings for up to 128 channels in a single request, instead of paging through them. A guide with thousands of channels fills in faster, and channels whose guide source is down show as temporarily unavailable rather than empty.
- **Signing in to Vela lasts a year.** An app sign-in stays valid for a year and renews whenever the app is used within 90 days, up from a 90-day limit with 30 days of inactivity. Silo app sign-ins follow the same rule.
- **Lower-quality fallbacks are labelled correctly.** A lower-resolution version Ether re-encodes is labelled a transcode, not a remux.
